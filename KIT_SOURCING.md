# Kit sourcing: buttons and cut cards for 8 mats

Research for restocking the society's table kit: dealer buttons, all-in buttons
and cut cards for eight playing surfaces, delivered to Dublin. Cards and mats
were already sorted. Priced 4 October 2026.

An interactive version of this, with a live basket, cost/time comparison and a
pre-purchase checklist, is **[`kit-sourcing.html`](kit-sourcing.html)**. Open
it in any browser.

Why those quantities: duty does not scale with units, so once the €3 per
category is spent, every extra set costs only goods plus VAT. Twelve sets is
eight in play and four spares, which covers a season of losses. Four packs of
cut cards is 16 in play and 24 spare, which matters because cut cards are
consumables and the cheap ones curl.

**Stop rule at checkout.** If the button set prices above €4.00, the gap to
Amazon UK closes to €2.51 on this basket and is no longer worth the wait. At
that point buy the two Amazon SKUs in the sources below instead.

## The thing that changed

On **1 July 2026** the EU scrapped the €150 duty-free threshold. Anything
arriving from outside the EU now attracts:

- **€3 customs duty per distinct product type** in the parcel. Two product
  types means €6, whether you buy two units or two hundred. Revenue's wording is
  "each distinct product type in a package", so identical items count once.
- **23% Irish VAT**, charged on the goods *and* the duty.
- **€6.95 An Post handling** if nobody paid the duty at checkout. Amazon always
  does. AliExpress and Temu collect VAT via IOSS but not reliably the duty.

The measure is temporary until July 2028, and a further **€2 per-parcel EU
handling fee is expected from November 2026**.

Two consequences that drive everything below:

1. **Amazon.co.uk is an import.** Great Britain is outside the EU, so a UK order
   carries the same duty. Amazon shows it at checkout as "Import Fees" and pays
   it for you, so there's no An Post ambush, however it is real money.
2. **Duty doesn't scale with quantity.** Ordering 10 of something costs the same
   €3 as ordering 1. Import overheads amortise; a bigger order is a cheaper
   order per unit.

### Why the EU column is so bad

Not the buttons and not the cut cards. **There is no cheap EU-domestic all-in
button.** The cheapest found was €7.95 ceramic. At 10 units that's €79.50 of a
€156 total. Drop the all-in buttons and the EU route falls to about €77, which
is defensible.

### Where the breakeven sits

The China unit price is the weakest input in the whole model, so this is a
sensitivity, not a verdict:

| China price per 4-pc set | Landed, clean | Landed, +An Post | Saving vs Amazon.co.uk |
|---|---|---|---|
| €2.00 | €41 | €48 | €25 / €18 |
| €2.50 | €47 | €54 | €19 / €12 |
| €3.00 | €54 | €61 | €13 / €6 |
| €3.50 | €60 | €67 | €7 / **-€0.33** |
| €4.00 | €66 | €73 | €0.47 / **-€6** |

**Breakeven is around €3.50 a set.** Above that, with any An Post involvement,
China is both more expensive and slower, with no practical returns route. Build
the real basket and read the actual price before deciding.

## What to order

Two product types, deliberately, because a third adds €3 of duty before it adds
anything useful.

### 1. Four-piece acrylic button set × 10

Dealer, small blind, big blind and all-in in one SKU. Buying all-in buttons
separately is where every route goes wrong; standalone ones run €7.95 to €14.95.

**Insist on:**

- **50 mm diameter.** Players' consensus for home and club play, visible from
  every seat without dominating the pot. The 76 mm (3") pucks are card-room kit.
- **Acrylic, not plastic.** Moulded "plastic" buttons are widely reported as
  flimsy and prone to breaking.
- **Engraved or inlaid, double-sided.** Surface printing wears off a button
  handled every hand.

**Price:** €2.50 to €4.00. **Quantity:** 10 for 8 mats, because buttons walk.

**The trap:** 30 mm sets. The cheap listings that flood these searches are
chip-sized discs with "DEALER" printed on them, not dealer buttons. Read the
spec table, not the title.

**Honest about quality:** a 50 mm acrylic button at this price is 3-6 mm thick
and weighs 10-15 g, against a 20-45 g comfort band. Light. On a *mat* that
matters much less than on a bare table, which is the setup here. Ceramic at
about €6.20 a set feels noticeably better and is the only upgrade worth
considering.

### 2. Cut cards, pack of 10 × 2

**Insist on 63-64 × 88 mm, poker size.** Our decks are poker size (63 mm).
Bridge-size cut cards are 57 mm, six millimetres narrower, and leave the bottom
card visible at both edges, which defeats the only job a cut card has.

**Price:** €3.00 to €5.00 a pack. **Quantity:** 20 cards, since two decks per mat in
rotation is 16 in play, and cut cards get shuffled into decks and lost.

**The trap:** bridge size sold under a "poker cut card" title. Separately, cheap
cut cards curl and bow, and this is the most common complaint across budget
suppliers. The fix is buying spares, not buying better. Store them flat.

### Not worth ordering from China

- **A dealer button with a built-in timer.** Most likely item to arrive dead,
  with no realistic warranty route. (We have `Timer_v2.html` anyway.)
- **Anything to reach a shipping threshold.** There isn't one.
- **A third product category.** A €4 discard tray costs €7 once duty lands. Add
  more of what you're already buying instead.

## Before paying

- [ ] **One seller, one basket.** Separate sellers ship separate consignments,
      each assessed separately and each able to draw its own €6.95.
- [ ] **Cut cards read 63-64 × 88 mm in the spec table**, not just "poker" in
      the title. This is the error that makes the order useless.
- [ ] **Button sets read 50 mm, acrylic.**
- [ ] **Duty shown at checkout**, not left to An Post.
- [ ] **A real Irish delivery date** on the listing. "Ships worldwide" is not a
      date.
- [ ] **Spare quantity decided.** Going 10 → 20 sets adds goods but €0 of duty.
- [ ] **Ordered**, before November, to stay ahead of the €2-per-parcel fee.

## Where this could be wrong

- **The China unit price is the weakest input.** An earlier pass used €1.90,
  which turned out to be the price of the 30 mm sets. Corrected to €3.00, and
  the verdict moves with it.
- **VAT-inclusive or not?** AliExpress shows EU visitors VAT-inclusive prices;
  US-facing search results are not. Which one you read moves the China column
  by about 20%.
- **Per category or per parcel?** Revenue says per distinct product type; at
  least one trade source reads it as per parcel. Revenue governs what you pay,
  so this model follows Revenue.
- **The EU column is the least-evidenced.** Amazon.ie's catalogue is thin here
  and it is priced off a handful of listings. A cheaper Irish seller may exist.
- **Prices drift**, Amazon's fastest of all.
