Buttons — the honest version

A 50 mm acrylic button at this price is 3–6 mm thick and weighs 10–15 g, against a 20–45 g comfort band. Light. On a mat that matters much less than on a bare table, which is the setup here. Ceramic at ~€6.20 a set feels better and is the only upgrade worth considering.

Not worth ordering from China

  • A dealer button with a built-in timer — most likely item to arrive dead, no realistic warranty route.
  • Anything to reach a shipping threshold. There isn’t one.
  • A third product category. A €4 discard tray costs €7 once duty lands. Add more of what you’re already buying instead.

Why a bigger order is a cheaper order

Duty is charged once per product type, not per unit. Ten button sets pay the same €3 as one.

The rules, and where this could be wrong

What changed on 1 July 2026

The EU scrapped the €150 duty-free threshold. Revenue charges €3 per distinct product type in a parcel from outside the EU, and 23% VAT on goods and that duty. Amazon.co.uk counts as outside the EU; Amazon pays it at checkout. AliExpress and Temu collect VAT via IOSS but not reliably the duty — when they don’t, An Post holds the parcel and charges €6.95 to process it. Temporary until July 2028.

Known soft spots

  • The China unit price is the weakest input, which is why it has its own control. An earlier version used €1.90 — the price of the 30 mm sets, not the 50 mm ones. Breakeven against Amazon.co.uk sits near €3.50 a set.
  • VAT-inclusive or not? AliExpress shows EU visitors VAT-inclusive prices; US-facing search results are not. Which one you read moves the China column ~20%.
  • Per category or per parcel? Revenue says per distinct product type; one trade source reads it as per parcel. Revenue governs what you pay.
  • The EU column is the least-evidenced. Amazon.ie’s catalogue is thin here and this is priced off a handful of listings.
  • A €2 EU handling fee per parcel is expected from November 2026. Not modelled; ordering now dodges it.
